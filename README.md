# My STEM Capstone Project 
[https://sites.google.com/jeffcoschools.us/portermitchelmore-stemcapstone/home-page-and-introductionproject-summary](url)

Abstract: 
Microscopic fatigue cracks in aerospace aluminum alloys represent a persistent challenge and structural hazard, particularly in aging commercial aircraft fleets where they are exposed to cyclic loading, leading to accelerated crack initiation and propagation. Traditional nondestructive testing techniques and methods, such as Eddy Current Testing (ECT), rely heavily on manual human interpretation of the impedance-plane graphs that are output. This process is time-intensive, operator-dependent, and prone to oversight, especially when the signals are subtle or obscured by outside noise. This study aims to investigate whether a convolutional neural network (CNN) can automate the classification process with the ECT graphs with accuracy comparable to, or even exceeding, human inspection, thereby addressing a critical gap in automated NDT signal interpretation. A dataset of over 4800 ECT impedance-plane graph images tested on aluminum alloys, with a split of 75% real world and 25% synthetic, was preprocessed through a standardized pipeline and used to train a custom, small CNN architecture across five independent random seeds to ensure robustness and replicability. This small CNN model achieved a test accuracy of .99, macro-precision accuracy of .99, recall of .99, F1-score of .99, and an area under the ROC curve (AUC) of .990. The confusion matrix, consisting of 289 TN, 2 FP, 6 FN, and 576 TP, demonstrated strong robustness and performance across both classes, with minimal misclassification. These results indicate that deep learning can reliably interpret these ECT graphs and signals consistently, while also reducing inspection time significantly. This substantially reduces human error in microcrack detection. The findings contribute a new understanding to the intersection of materials science, aerospace engineering, and machine learning by demonstrating that automated ECT interpretation is both feasible and highly accurate. Implications of this include improved aviation safety for everyone, reduced inspection time, and the potential for completely autonomous NDT pipelines for future applications. Limitations include dataset constraints and the absence of real-time deployment and testing applications. Future research should integrate different alloys to enforce robustness, expand on real-world datasets, and evaluate other more advanced architectures, such as ResNet18, including other transformer-based vision models as well.
